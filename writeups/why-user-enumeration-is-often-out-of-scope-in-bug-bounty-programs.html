Why user enumeration is often out of scope in bug bounty programs?

Published: 12.08.2026

As of me - bad design, security treated as an afterthought, negligence. Companies would not pay a bounty and fix an issue that requires redesign or refactoring.

I asked Gemini the same question and this is the answer:

User enumeration is often out of scope in bug bounty programs because it generates low-value noise, clogs system logs, and risks user privacy or performance issues without proving a severe security flaw.

Why It Is Out of Scope

  • High System Noise: Automated checks send many requests and fill server logs.
  • Low Security Impact: It rarely leads to direct system compromise on its own.
  • Privacy and Compliance Risk: Testing account lists can touch real user data.
  • Denial of Service Risk: Rapid lookup requests can slow down or crash the login page.

As an ethical hacker I understand these concerns, but companies are trying to protect their assets from malicious actors, right? Why would a malicious actor care about these?
